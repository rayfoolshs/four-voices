The languages under the language

Every word has a passport.

English is a collision of older languages. Change the root stock and you change the temperature of the poem. Search the hoard, or start with a family.

A working hoard, not the Oxford English Dictionary. Etymologies are simplified and sometimes contested; treat them as prompts, not proofs.

Method

How this reads you.

Four Voices is a workshop, not an oracle. It listens for the things a good reader notices first — where the beat falls, where the line turns, which older language a word came from — and then it asks you to make one deliberate change.

The four lenses

Heaney for weight and ground: four or five stresses, monosyllables, a caesura where the tool strikes. Gorman for breath and public architecture: long lines, anaphora, the tricolon. Dylan for propulsion: the fourteener, the rhyme as engine, the plain line that refuses. Bilston for friction: strict form, supermarket diction, deadpan feeling.

The engine, honestly

Syllables and stress are estimated from spelling and a working dictionary of common words. Rhymes are approximate sound families. Etymology is a hand-built lexicon of a few hundred words, with suffix guesses for the rest. This is a second ear, not a substitute for yours. When the machine and your ear disagree, the ear wins.

The coach is deterministic: it reads the analysis of your actual draft and responds to what you ask. If you add a model key in Settings, free-form chat is sent to that model instead; the draft and analysis go with it. Nothing is sent anywhere unless you turn that on.

Privacy

On imitation

The "pastiche" in each voice drawer is original writing in the manner of that poet — not a quotation. Imitating a living poet's style for practice is old and honourable; publishing a close imitation as your own voice is neither. Steal the rhythm, not the life.
